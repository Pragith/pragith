Often I wondered whether I am an introvert or an extrovert. Sometimes there are situations where you HAVE TO BE shy, like when your parents take you to their friends' place. When they offer you something to eat, YOU HAVE TO reject at least the first time (this is what I feel). And this kind of feeling, I put under Generation 2.

These days I have been observing the kids in my apartment and my college friends. I categorize them as Generation 3. Their primary language is English. I am going to be a little blunt here  -  no matter whether they want to or not, their parents too (who again may or may not want to) speak in English. Forced by the community? Maybe!

Their sentences like, "You have that book, no?", "You mad, huh?" The use of words like "Da", which means "Dude" in Tamil/Kannada. Using words like "Guys" even for girls, when there is already "Gals". Using "Dude" instead of "Dudette".

Trying to speak in English has made others tag them "South Indians", and this is precisely why people make fun of us.

Many times, I have been called "Uncle" by these kids. Why? Because there is no such word as "Bhaiyya" or "Anna" in their vocabulary. They can't call me "Bro" or "Dude" because they don't know me well  -  they use those only with people who are close to them and of the same age.

Personally speaking, I am an extrovert with these kids, but how should I behave when I meet older people or my classmates?

1. In which language should I speak? English or their mother tongue? Speaking English will make them feel as if I am trying to act smart (YES! At least that's what I feel when someone is speaking to me in English even after knowing that we both know our mother tongue very well).

2. Should I take what is offered to me right away, or should I wait for the next turn?

As Willy Shakey once wrote for Hamlet, "To be or not to be, that is the question." Where I fall is still a jigsaw puzzle to me.
